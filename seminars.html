Speaker: Zibo Zhang (Tianjin University)

Time: 4 December, 2026, 11:30 a.m. - 12:30 p.m.

Location: Zoom

Title: From second moments to pairwise negative correlation: Applications to uniform and minimum spanning trees.

Abstract: A spanning tree connects all the vertices of a graph without creating a cycle. We consider two natural random models: a uniform spanning tree, chosen uniformly from all spanning trees, and a minimum spanning tree, which minimizes the total edge weight when the edges receive independent random weights with a common continuous distribution. A classical property of uniform spanning trees is pairwise negative correlation: knowing that one edge is present cannot increase the probability that another edge is present. This holds on every finite connected graph. For minimum spanning trees, however, it can fail, as shown by Lyons, Peres and Schramm. In this talk, I will explain how counting pairs of edges connects negative correlation with the second moment of vertex degrees. We use this connection to prove negative correlation for disjoint edges in minimum spanning trees on sufficiently large complete graphs. We also answer a question posed by Nachmias and Peres. For a uniform spanning tree on a finite connected simple regular graph, let D be the tree degree of a vertex chosen uniformly and independently of the tree. They asked whether there exists a universal constant C such that $\mathbb{E}[D^2] ≤ C$, for every such graph. We answer this question affirmatively and determine the optimal constant: $\mathbb{E}[D^2] < 6$, and six cannot be replaced by any smaller universal constant. The talk will introduce basic definitions and examples before presenting the results and proof ideas. This is joint work with Pengfei Tang.

Speaker: Mark Pollicott (University of Warwick)

Time: 24 November, 2026, 1:00 - 2:00 p.m.

Location: MAC D109

Title: Gibbs measures in a cold climate.

Abstract: One of the many interesting ideas which has been imported into Dynamical Systems from Statistical Mechanics is that of Gibbs measures.  These are families of measures which typically behave well in terms of the dynamics.  Since they originated in Statistical Mechanics there is a natural parameter called the "inverse temperature" $1/T$ and it is natural to consider the limiting behaviour of the measures as the "temperature"  tends to zero (i.e., the cold climate).  In some cases the limit was described by a result of Bremont in 2003.  We will describe a new elementary proof.  This is joint work with Oliver Jenkinson (started in 2007 and finished in October 2026).

Speaker: Manan Bhatia (University of Geneva)

Time: 20 November, 2026, 11:30 a.m. - 12:30 p.m.

Location: Zoom

Title: Geodesic switches and exceptional times in dynamical last passage percolation.

Abstract: Exponential last passage percolation (LPP) is a canonical model of random geometry in the KPZ universality class. In dynamical LPP, the weights are independently resampled over time, causing geodesics to evolve. We will study how much geodesics change over a time interval and how these changes control the trace swept out by the evolving paths. We will also investigate the possibility of exceptional times admitting nontrivial bi-infinite geodesics, which are almost surely absent in the static model. Our main focus will be Brownian LPP with discrete resampling dynamics, where estimates on “geodesic switches” yield a Hausdorff-dimension upper bound of $1/2$ for the exceptional time set. We will explain the mechanism behind these estimates and their connection to exceptional times. We will conclude by briefly discussing a subsequent improvement of the bound to zero and the remaining question of whether exceptional times exist. The talk will introduce the model and the relevant background.

Title: An ergodic approach to equations of the form $x+y=\lfloor \alpha(n)\rfloor$.

Abstract: Khalfalah and Szemerédi proved in 2006 that every finite coloring of the positive integers contains distinct monochromatic integers $x$ and $y$ such that $x+y=n^2$, for some natural $n$. In this talk, we will discuss an ergodic approach to equations of the form $x+y=\lfloor \alpha(n)\rfloor$, where $\alpha(x)$ is a Hardy field function of polynomial growth, such as $x^7\log(x), x^3+\log(x)^2$ or $x^\pi$. We will see when such equations have solutions with $x,y$ lying either in the same cell of a given finite partition of the positive integers or in a given set of positive upper density. This is joint work with Vitaly Bergelson and Hao Pan.
