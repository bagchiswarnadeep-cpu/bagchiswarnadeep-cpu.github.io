Loop Contracting Random Walk

This is a new stochastic process introduced for the first time in a joint work of Gourab Ray and Arnab Sen , titled Loop Contracting Random Walk.

This process is a natural analogue of the loop-erased random walk (which is used to sample the uniform spanning tree). However, instead of erasing loops, the process contracts them.

The process is animated here on a smaller grid. Animation credit: Gourab Ray.

An open question is to prove that this walk is transient on the Euclidean lattice. This has several consequences for the existence of weak limits of the minimum spanning arborescence.

How CLEB behaves locally

CLEB is an algorithm to sample Minimum Spanning Arborescence (MSA) on a finite oriented graph. We used CLEB to identify the local weak limit of MSA on complete directed graph with i.i.d. exponential edge weights in this paper. Simulation credit: GPT-6.2 Astra.

How super vertices grow during CLEB: snowball effect

Here we can see that the sizes of super vertices grow eventually. This snowball effect is exploited a lot to identify local limit of MSA. Simulation credit: GPT-6.2 Astra.

Weight of MSA

Let $W_n^*$ be the weight of MSA on complete directed graph and $W_n$ be the sum of the weights of all minimal outgoing edges from all the vertices except the fixed boundary vertex $\partial$ of complete directed graph with $n$ vertices. Simulations indicate that $\mathbb{E}(W_n^* - W_n)=O(1/n)$, but in this paper, we are able to show that $\mathbb{E}(W_n^* - W_n)=o(1/\sqrt{n})$.
